Six-month forecast

How the budget becomes a weekly cash flow

The budget template feeds a model that works out when each figure turns into cash, and writes the answer back into the same workbook.

01  /  The model

Budget in, timing out

The budget sheets are the input. Python turns each monthly figure into dated amounts, and those go back into Excel as the cash flow tabs.

02  /  Revenue

Four steps from a month to a date

Each step moves the same money further right. Nothing is added or lost along the way — only the date changes.

03  /  COGS

The same steps, one lever short

Identical machinery, pointed outbound.

There is no step 4 on this side. The late buffer is set to zero, so COGS is assumed to be paid on the due date — a placeholder, not a policy. Turning it on is a number per provider, not a rebuild.

04  /  Factoring

A slice of revenue that skips the wait

Where a bank buys the invoices, most of the money arrives on a weekly rhythm instead of on the customer’s terms.

Set in Excel, not in the model. Which countries use a facility, what share of revenue goes through it, the advance and the fee — four cells per country. Today only Denmark is switched on, at 40%.

05  /  Manpower and SG&A

No terms, just a day of the month

These twelve lines never wait for anybody. Each carries a rule, and the rule is a dropdown per country.

Bad debt is the exception: it is set to no cash effect, because it is written off rather than paid.

06  /  What comes out

A cash flow per country, week by week

Three years, because December 2027 invoicing on 60-day terms is 2028 cash. Weeks sit under their month and collapse into it, so the same tab is a weekly and a monthly view.

07  /  Not in it yet

What the model does not do

Worth saying out loud, because each one changes the number on the page.

No opening cash balance

It shows the movement in and out of each week, not the balance in the account. Adding a starting figure per country turns it into a position.

No VAT

Amounts are net. VAT moves real cash on its own cycle and is not in here.

One currency

Everything is stated in DKK. The rate a country’s budget was translated at is applied before a figure is typed, so nothing here can restate it.

No financing or investment

Operating cash only. Loans, capex and interest sit outside the model for now.
